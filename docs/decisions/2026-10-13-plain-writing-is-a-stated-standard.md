# Plain writing is a stated standard in docs-conventions, not a rule in every skill

## Context
The tells of machine writing, announcing openers, hedges and intensifiers, jargon, rhetorical setups, em dashes, creep into the free text the pack writes: a Why, a decision's context, a pull request. The stop-slop skill lists them well; it also bans every adverb and scores prose on a rubric. The case written for this, a Why rewritten from a request full of tells, passed before the rule existed, with the plugin and without it: the templates' limits already strip most of it.

## Options
- A rule in each of the eight skills: lost, eight copies of the same text.
- Bundling stop-slop: lost, it stacks on its own and is aimed at articles.
- One Writing section in docs-conventions, the practice every command loads, in our words, credited: won.
- No rule, since the case passed: lost, by the owner's call; the standard also governs the pack's own files and the contributors who write them.

## Decision
docs-conventions carries a Writing section: plain words, no announcing opener, no hedge or intensifier, no jargon, no em dash, no rhetorical setup, active voice, one idea per sentence, specifics over adjectives, with the template's limit as the length.

## Consequences
Why: one place, loaded by every command, is the only way a writing rule stays consistent and cheap.
Revisit when: a case shows the tells getting through with the rule in place; then the rule gets sharper, or the template's limit does.
